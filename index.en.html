What are we really longing for?

Lost Dreams is a narrative exploration game set in a Chinese family home at the turn of the millennium. You return to a room from memory — touching old belongings, switching on the computer, listening to the voices on the TV — and over three nights you drift into three different dreams.

As children, we couldn’t wait to grow up; once grown, we began to miss the past. The people in the dreams carry what we imagined the future would be at different ages, while the era we look back on slowly fills with things that never belonged to it. So the game asks: is it an era we long for, or a version of ourselves that never has to face the present?

Explore old belongings, talk with the people in your dreams, and draw a fish of your own. As the three nights unfold, the light, the sounds and the familiar objects begin to show cracks. In the end, you leave the old home by the stairs and look back once more at the dream you once had: the fish and the memories stay in the dream, and you return to today.

How technology shapes the story

The whole work takes place in one old home, where the light, the sound, the objects and the computer interface change across the three nights. The fish you draw with your own hand swims into the dream, turning memory from an image you look at into something you take part in. Here, technology is what organises time and memory: a familiar space slowly turns strange, until you finally leave the past you built yourself.

AI assistance and human authorship

I began from a longing for the past, an imagined future, and the urge to escape the present, and from there I conceived the story and interactions of the three nights, adjusting the visuals and the experience through repeated playtesting. During production I used ChatGPT/Codex and Claude Code to help write, debug and integrate code; I also wrote Blender scripts to assist with 3D modelling, material tweaks and scene building, and used generative image tools for some visual assets and candidate designs. All of this was selected and reworked by me before it became part of the game.
